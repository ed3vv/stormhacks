S³ Golf · map concepts 3 & 4

Coral Cove & Aurora Basin

Two more maps for the 3-sphere course, alongside Meadow and Desert Oasis. Every frame below is drawn with the game's own S³ projection, so it is what the camera would actually see. The ground curves up into a bowl, things past a quarter lap grow as they recede, and whatever is overhead is the far side of the world.

Coral Cove

beach · late-morning sun · ocean overhead

A sandy cove wrapped around a turquoise sea. The ocean is a round cap of the ground sphere centred past the equator, so from the first tee the coastline sits mid-frame and the open sea hangs overhead. Palms, beach huts, a pier and a striped lighthouse frame the holes.

The S³ twist

Every hole has two routes: the short one over the water and the long one round the world on land.

On a 3-sphere the straight line from tee to cup keeps going and comes back. Hole 3 sits across the bay: carry the water for a birdie, or putt the other way round the 75 m circumference along the beach and never get wet.

Look straight up from any tee and you see the whole sea, because the ocean cap covers the antipode.

Aurora Basin

ice · low pale sun · aurora at the pole

A snowfield around a frozen lake, with snowy pines, crystal spires, an igloo and an ice castle. The aurora hangs in a loop 7–13 m up around the pole. The pole is straight up from every point on the ground, so the aurora is overhead wherever you stand.

The S³ twist

The lake has almost no friction, so a putt on the ice follows a great circle and can lap the whole world.

Rolling friction on the ice is about a tenth of grass. A firm putt that crosses the lake keeps going until it reaches snow. If it never does, it comes back to you from behind 75 m later.

The aurora marks the pole. Full power straight up passes through the curtain, crosses the pole and lands at your antipode. The curtain flashes when the ball goes through it.

Build plan

what each map needs in the Unity project

Shared groundwork

  1. Surface zones new: circles on the ground (ocean, lake, drifts, wet sand) registered like the desert oases. The shader colours them, and the ball physics reads the same list for friction and bounce, so what you see is what the ball feels.
  2. Water hazard new: on entering the ocean, splash, add 1 stroke and drop the ball at the crossing point.
  3. Emissive vertex flag new: unlit, bright colours for the lighthouse lamp and the aurora, so bloom makes them glow.
  4. Biome switch exists: M cycles four maps instead of two. Each map gets a Style, a post profile and a course.

Per map

  1. Coral Cove: water shading (depth gradient, animated foam line, sun glints), plus lighthouse, pier, huts, umbrellas, surfboards, sandcastles and gulls. Palms and rocks are reused.
  2. Aurora Basin: ice shading (cracks, glints), snow sparkle, the aurora loop mesh at the pole, falling snow, and castle, igloo, snowman, crystals and snowy pines. Crenels are reused from the fort.
  3. Performance: everything new bakes into the single scenery mesh, and water, ice and snow are ground-shader branches, so draw calls stay at about 20.

The frames above come from a small software version of the game's renderer: same exp map, SO(4) frames and view transform as the shader, with flat shading and painter's sorting in place of the GPU depth buffer.
